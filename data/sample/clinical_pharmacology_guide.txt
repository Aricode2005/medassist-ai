CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY REFERENCE GUIDE
========================================

METFORMIN HYDROCHLORIDE (Glucophage)
--------------------------------------

1. INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Metformin hydrochloride is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic
control in adults and pediatric patients 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

2. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
- Adults: Initial dose 500 mg orally twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals
- Maximum recommended daily dose: 2550 mg for adults
- Pediatric (10-16 years): Initial dose 500 mg twice daily, maximum 2000 mg/day
- Dose should be increased gradually to reduce gastrointestinal side effects

3. CONTRAINDICATIONS
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2)
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis

4. WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS

4.1 Lactic Acidosis
WARNING: Lactic acidosis is a rare but serious complication that can occur due to metformin
accumulation. The risk increases with conditions such as sepsis, dehydration, excess alcohol
intake, hepatic impairment, renal impairment, and acute congestive heart failure.
Symptoms include malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence, and nonspecific
abdominal distress. Laboratory findings include low pH, elevated blood lactate, and
increased anion gap.

4.2 Vitamin B12 Deficiency
Long-term treatment with metformin has been associated with decreased serum vitamin B12
levels. Periodic measurement of hematologic parameters and vitamin B12 levels is recommended,
particularly in patients with peripheral neuropathy or anemia.

4.3 Hypoglycemia
Metformin alone does not usually cause hypoglycemia, but it may occur when used in combination
with other glucose-lowering agents such as sulfonylureas or insulin.

5. DRUG INTERACTIONS

5.1 Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (e.g., Topiramate, Zonisamide)
Concurrent use may increase the risk of lactic acidosis. Monitor patients more frequently.

5.2 Cationic Drugs (e.g., Amiloride, Digoxin, Morphine, Quinidine, Ranitidine, Vancomycin)
These drugs may compete with metformin for renal tubular transport, potentially increasing
metformin plasma concentration. Monitor closely and adjust dose if necessary.

5.3 Alcohol
Alcohol potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism. Patients should be warned
against excessive alcohol intake while receiving metformin.

5.4 Iodinated Contrast Agents
Metformin should be temporarily discontinued at the time of or prior to iodinated contrast
imaging procedures in patients with eGFR between 30-60 mL/min/1.73 m2.

6. ADVERSE REACTIONS
Most common adverse reactions (incidence > 5%):
- Diarrhea (53.2%)
- Nausea/Vomiting (25.5%)
- Flatulence (12.1%)
- Abdominal discomfort (6.4%)
- Indigestion (7.1%)
- Asthenia (9.2%)
- Headache (5.7%)

ATORVASTATIN CALCIUM (Lipitor)
--------------------------------------

1. INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Atorvastatin is indicated as an adjunct to diet for treatment of elevated total cholesterol,
LDL-C, apolipoprotein B, and triglycerides in patients with primary hyperlipidemia and
mixed dyslipidemia. Also indicated for prevention of cardiovascular disease in patients
with multiple risk factors.

2. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
- Adults: Initial dose 10-20 mg once daily
- Patients requiring large LDL-C reduction (>45%): 40 mg once daily
- Maximum dose: 80 mg once daily
- Can be taken at any time of day, with or without food

3. CONTRAINDICATIONS
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of hepatic transaminase levels
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of this medication
- Pregnancy and lactation - may cause fetal harm

4. WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS

4.1 Skeletal Muscle Effects (Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis)
WARNING: Rare cases of rhabdomyolysis with acute renal failure secondary to myoglobinuria
have been reported. Risk factors include advanced age (>65), uncontrolled hypothyroidism,
renal impairment, and concomitant use of certain medications.
Patients should be advised to report promptly unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness.

4.2 Liver Enzyme Abnormalities
Increases in serum transaminases (AST/ALT) have been reported. Liver function tests should
be performed before initiating therapy and as clinically indicated thereafter.

4.3 Endocrine Function
Increases in HbA1c and fasting serum glucose levels have been reported with HMG-CoA
reductase inhibitors, including atorvastatin.

5. DRUG INTERACTIONS

5.1 Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Clarithromycin, Itraconazole, HIV Protease Inhibitors)
Concomitant use increases the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Avoid or limit atorvastatin
dose to 20 mg daily when used with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

5.2 Cyclosporine, Gemfibrozil, or Niacin
The risk of myopathy is increased. Atorvastatin dose should not exceed 10 mg daily when
used concomitantly with cyclosporine.

5.3 Warfarin
Atorvastatin may increase INR values in patients receiving warfarin. Monitor prothrombin time
before and frequently during early atorvastatin therapy.

5.4 Digoxin
Co-administration increases digoxin steady-state plasma concentrations by approximately 20%.
Monitor digoxin levels when initiating atorvastatin.

5.5 Oral Contraceptives
Co-administration increases norethindrone and ethinyl estradiol AUC values by approximately
30% and 20%, respectively. Consider these increases when selecting an oral contraceptive.

6. ADVERSE REACTIONS
Most common adverse reactions (incidence >= 2%):
- Nasopharyngitis (8.3%)
- Arthralgia (6.9%)
- Diarrhea (6.8%)
- Pain in extremity (6.0%)
- Urinary tract infection (5.7%)
- Dyspepsia (4.7%)
- Nausea (4.0%)
- Musculoskeletal pain (3.8%)
- Muscle spasms (3.6%)
- Myalgia (3.5%)
- Insomnia (3.0%)
- Pharyngolaryngeal pain (2.3%)

AMLODIPINE BESYLATE (Norvasc)
--------------------------------------

1. INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Amlodipine is a calcium channel blocker indicated for the treatment of:
- Hypertension (may be used alone or in combination with other antihypertensive agents)
- Chronic stable angina
- Vasospastic angina (Prinzmetal's angina)
- Angiographically documented coronary artery disease in patients without heart failure

2. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
- Hypertension: Initial dose 5 mg once daily, maximum 10 mg once daily
- Angina: 5-10 mg once daily
- Elderly or hepatic impairment: Initial dose 2.5 mg once daily
- Small, fragile patients: Initial dose 2.5 mg once daily

3. DRUG INTERACTIONS

3.1 Simvastatin
Co-administration with amlodipine increases exposure to simvastatin. Limit simvastatin
dose to 20 mg daily in patients taking amlodipine.

3.2 Cyclosporine
Co-administration increases cyclosporine exposure. Monitor cyclosporine levels and adjust
dose as necessary.

3.3 CYP3A4 Inhibitors
Co-administration with moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem, ketoconazole)
may increase amlodipine plasma concentrations. Monitor for symptoms of hypotension and edema.

4. ADVERSE REACTIONS
Most common adverse reactions (incidence > 2%):
- Edema (peripheral) (8.3%)
- Dizziness (3.4%)
- Flushing (2.6%)
- Palpitation (4.5%)
- Fatigue (4.5%)
- Nausea (2.9%)
- Abdominal pain (1.6%)
- Somnolence (1.4%)

CLINICAL GUIDELINES: TYPE 2 DIABETES MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------

SCREENING AND DIAGNOSIS
- Fasting plasma glucose >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions
- HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- 2-hour plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL during oral glucose tolerance test
- Random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL with classic hyperglycemia symptoms

TREATMENT ALGORITHM

Step 1: Lifestyle Modifications
- Medical nutrition therapy and weight management
- Physical activity: minimum 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity exercise
- Diabetes self-management education and support (DSMES)

Step 2: First-Line Pharmacotherapy
- Metformin is recommended as first-line therapy unless contraindicated
- Starting dose: 500 mg twice daily with meals
- Titrate over 1-2 months to minimize GI side effects

Step 3: Combination Therapy (if HbA1c target not achieved after 3 months)
Add one of the following to metformin:
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., Semaglutide, Liraglutide) - preferred if CVD or weight concerns
- SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin) - preferred if heart failure or CKD
- DPP-4 inhibitors (e.g., Sitagliptin, Saxagliptin)
- Sulfonylureas (e.g., Glimepiride, Glipizide) - if cost is a concern
- Thiazolidinediones (e.g., Pioglitazone)
- Basal insulin (e.g., Insulin Glargine, Insulin Detemir)

Step 4: Intensification
- Consider triple therapy or insulin-based regimens
- If on basal insulin, add prandial insulin (basal-bolus regimen)

GLYCEMIC TARGETS
- General adult population: HbA1c < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol)
- Elderly/frail or high hypoglycemia risk: HbA1c < 8.0% (64 mmol/mol)
- Younger patients without complications: HbA1c < 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Pre-prandial glucose: 80-130 mg/dL (4.4-7.2 mmol/L)
- Peak postprandial glucose: < 180 mg/dL (< 10.0 mmol/L)

MONITORING
- HbA1c: Every 3 months until stable, then every 6 months
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) as recommended
- Annual comprehensive metabolic panel
- Annual lipid panel
- Annual microalbumin/creatinine ratio
- Eye exam: At diagnosis and annually thereafter
- Foot exam: At every visit
- Blood pressure: At every visit, target < 130/80 mmHg

CARDIOVASCULAR RISK MANAGEMENT
- Statin therapy recommended for most adults with diabetes aged 40-75
- Aspirin 75-162 mg/day for secondary prevention
- ACE inhibitor or ARB for patients with albuminuria
- Blood pressure target: < 130/80 mmHg
