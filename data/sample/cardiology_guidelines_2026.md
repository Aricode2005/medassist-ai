# Cardiology Clinical Practice Guidelines 2026

## 1. Management of Hypertension

### 1.1 First-line Therapies
- **ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril, Enalapril):** Recommended for non-black patients with diabetes or chronic kidney disease. 
  - *Contraindications:* Pregnancy, history of angioedema, bilateral renal artery stenosis.
  - *Common adverse effects:* Dry cough (switch to ARB if persistent), hyperkalemia.
- **Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Amlodipine):** Recommended for black patients and elderly patients.
  - *Common adverse effects:* Peripheral edema, flushing, palpitations.
- **Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., Chlorthalidone, Hydrochlorothiazide):** Highly effective for systolic hypertension in older adults.
  - *Monitor:* Potassium depletion, hyperuricemia (risk of gout flare).

### 1.2 Drug Interactions (Hypertension)
- **ACE Inhibitors + NSAIDs:** Concurrent use reduces the antihypertensive effect and increases the risk of acute renal failure.
- **ACE Inhibitors + Potassium-sparing diuretics:** High risk of severe hyperkalemia.

## 2. Heart Failure (HFrEF) Protocol

### 2.1 Core Medication Regimen
For Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction (LVEF < 40%), the foundational therapy includes:
1. **Beta-blockers (Carvedilol, Metoprolol Succinate, Bisoprolol)**
   - Start low and go slow. Do not initiate during acute decompensation.
2. **ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan - Entresto)**
   - Requires a 36-hour washout period after stopping an ACE inhibitor before initiation due to the high risk of angioedema.
3. **SGLT2 Inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin)**
   - Recommended regardless of diabetes status. 
   - *Warning:* Risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and genital mycotic infections.

### 2.2 Contraindications in Heart Failure
- **Non-DHP Calcium Channel Blockers (Verapamil, Diltiazem):** Strictly contraindicated in HFrEF due to negative inotropic effects (can worsen heart failure).
- **NSAIDs:** Avoid due to sodium/water retention and blunting of diuretic efficacy.
